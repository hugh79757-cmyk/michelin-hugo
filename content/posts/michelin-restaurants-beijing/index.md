## Where to Eat

### Xin Rong Ji (Xinyuan South Road)
Xin Rong Ji (Xinyuan South Road) holds 3 Stars for its Taizhou cuisine. The dining classification is Very Expensive, with costs running over ¥20,000. This flagship of the chain has an elegant but relaxed dining space done out in modern Chinese style. The restaurant showcases top-tier culinary standards in Beijing through its refined approach to regional flavors and high-end ingredients.

### Chao Shang Chao (Chaoyang)
Chao Shang Chao (Chaoyang) is awarded 3 Stars for its Chao Zhou cuisine. This upmarket restaurant falls under the Very Expensive category, requiring over ¥20,000. Upon arrival at this upmarket restaurant, diners step into a hallway lined with steeply priced dried goods, setting the stage for an opulent and authentic Chaozhou culinary experience.

### Jingji
Jingji earns 2 Stars for its Beijing Cuisine. Priced in the Very Expensive bracket at over ¥20,000, Jingji specialises in royal cuisine that not only pays homage to the nation's culinary traditions, but also executes them with precise modern techniques suited for elite palates in the capital.

### King's Joy
King's Joy holds 2 Stars for its Vegetarian cuisine. Operating in the Very Expensive tier at over ¥20,000, this historical courtyard house-turned-restaurant has a muted decor inspired by nature, offering guests an exceptionally serene environment adjacent to Yonghe Temple while serving meat-free dishes.

### [Shanghai](https://tour.techpawz.com/posts/shanghai-travel-guide/) Cuisine
Shanghai Cuisine is decorated with 2 Stars for its Shanghainese food. Categorized as Expensive, the bill ranges from ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. The cosy, understated space is done out in neutral greys with pops of teal green, providing a sophisticated backdrop where the Shanghainese home-style and classic recipes shine.

### Blackswan
Blackswan achieves 2 Stars for its French Contemporary cuisine. It sits in the Very Expensive price bracket at over ¥20,000. On a swan theme, feather motifs and graceful arabesques abound in the spotless white room of this restaurant, delivering an artistic and avant-garde Western dining event.

### Lamdre
Lamdre is recognized with 2 Stars for its Vegetarian menu. Dining here is Very Expensive, totaling over ¥20,000. A skylight in the pitched roof bathes the main room in natural light; the private rooms and balcony enhance the plant-based dining journey with architectural grace.

### Lu Shang Lu
Lu Shang Lu receives 2 Stars for its Shandong cuisine. This Very Expensive dining room exceeds ¥20,000 in cost. The speciality here is Shandong cooking, especially Confucius cuisine, sea cucumber and live seafood, all prepared with meticulous technique in an upscale setting.

### Huaiyang Fu (Dongcheng)
Huaiyang Fu (Dongcheng) claims 1 Star for its Huaiyang menu. Classified as Moderate, prices range between ¥3,000 and ¥8,000. This period mansion, complete with a stone garden and carved wooden windows, provides the perfect formal backdrop for delicate and sweet-leaning regional Chinese dishes.

### Chao Shang Chao (Xicheng)
Chao Shang Chao (Xicheng) holds 1 Star for its Chao Zhou menu. It falls into the Expensive bracket, charging between ¥8,000 and ¥20,000. This upmarket restaurant in Chaozhou is another gem from the F&B chain, where Chef Gu, who hails from the region, crafts faithful specialties.

### Seventh Son
Seventh Son secures 1 Star for Cantonese cooking. The pricing is Expensive, falling between ¥8,000 and ¥20,000. This [Hong Kong](https://visafree.techpawz.com/posts/hong-kong-visa-free/) brand, famous for both gourmet and home-style fare, reopened its Beijing outpost to deliver time-tested Cantonese classics and dim sum favorites.

### Zijin Mansion
Zijin Mansion is awarded 1 Star for its Cantonese offerings. Operating in the Expensive tier of ¥8,000 to ¥20,000, though its name means "purple and gold", this cosy restaurant is clad in hand-embroidered orange fabric, creating a warm ambiance for refined southern Chinese plates.

### Xin Rong Ji (Jinrong Street)
Xin Rong Ji (Jinrong Street) holds 1 Star for Taizhou cuisine. Prices are Expensive, ranging from ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. This is the first Beijing branch of the Taizhou brand famous for super-fresh, top-notch seafood shipped directly from its coastal origins.

### The Georg
The Georg achieves 1 Star for European Contemporary cuisine. This Very Expensive destination charges over ¥20,000. The three-storey complex with an art gallery, a main dining room and a space for private banquets boasts minimalist Scandinavian design alongside precise, creative plates.

### Trb Hutong
Trb Hutong earns 1 Star for French Contemporary cooking. It is classified as Very Expensive at over ¥20,000. Hidden in the courtyard of a historic temple, this cosy, modern, professionally run dining room raises European gastronomy to high art against a traditional backdrop.

### The House of Dynasties
The House of Dynasties features 1 Star for Cantonese dishes. The price is Expensive, between ¥8,000 and ¥20,000. Inspired by Dream of the Red Chamber, an 18C Chinese novel, the decor embodies that era's charm, matching the literary thematic depth of its Cantonese menu.

### Zhiguan Courtyard
Zhiguan Courtyard holds 1 Star for Dongbei cuisine. This Expensive restaurant ranges from ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. This restaurant, annexed to an art gallery, sports a dining room complete with historical garden views that complement its robust northeastern Chinese flavors.

### Cai Yi Xuan
Cai Yi Xuan claims 1 Star for Cantonese plates. Costs run Expensive at ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. Of art garden inspiration, the tasteful dining room boasts an upmarket Chinese décor and soft lighting, perfectly suiting its delicate dim sum and premium seafood.

### Rong Pao
Rong Pao is awarded 1 Star for Sichuan fare. It falls into the Expensive range of ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. This brand under the Xin Rong Ji group specialises in Sichuan fare, in particular cuisine from Yibin, offering spicy and complex flavor profiles.

### Xin Rong Ji (Jianguomenwai Street)
Xin Rong Ji (Jianguomenwai Street) holds 1 Star for Taizhou cooking. Pricing is Expensive, spanning ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. The upmarket chain is famous for flawlessly fresh seafood flown in from Taizhou daily and this branch upholds that exact standard.

### Poetry‧Wine (Dongsanhuan Middle Road)
Poetry‧Wine (Dongsanhuan Middle Road) achieves 1 Star for Beijing Cuisine. It is categorized as Moderate, priced between ¥3,000 and ¥8,000. The bamboo bush, jade ornaments, ink paintings and cut flowers may prime your brain for a hefty price, yet it remains relatively accessible for royal-style dining.

### The Beijing Kitchen (Jianguo Road)
The Beijing Kitchen (Jianguo Road) secures 1 Star for Cantonese fare. This Moderate restaurant charges between ¥3,000 and ¥8,000. With over 40 years of experience, this owner-chef from Hong Kong samples what his team makes daily to ensure absolute quality.

### Sheng Yong Xing (Chaoyang)
Sheng Yong Xing (Chaoyang) is recognized with 1 Star for Beijing Cuisine. It is an Expensive restaurant costing ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. It's all about the signature roast duck here; yours will even come with a 'birth certificate' proving its lineage and preparation metrics.

### Jing
Jing earns 1 Star for French Contemporary food. It is classified as Very Expensive, exceeding ¥20,000. What this basement restaurant space lacks in views it more than makes up for with eclectic art and a sophisticated menu of western fine dining creations.

### Mansion Cuisine by Jingyan
Mansion Cuisine by Jingyan holds 1 Star for Beijing Cuisine. It sits in the Very Expensive tier at over ¥20,000. A remodelled, high-ceilinged courtyard mansion houses this stunning restaurant whose splendid view creates a standout atmosphere for traditional northern tasting menus.

### Furong
Furong is awarded 1 Star for Hunanese cooking. The restaurant is priced as Expensive, between ¥8,000 and ¥20,000. Named after the provincial flower of Hunan, this brand is part of the Xin Rong Ji Group and serves fiery, aromatic regional specialties.

### Lu Style (Anding Road)
Lu Style (Anding Road) claims 1 Star for Shandong cuisine. Prices fall in the Expensive tier of ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. Instead of typical Shandong classics, the chef, who hails from the province, champions a menu starring innovative interpretations of coastal and inland traditions.

### Lei Garden (Jinbao Tower)
Lei Garden (Jinbao Tower) secures 1 Star for Cantonese cuisine. This Expensive establishment charges ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. Lei Garden hails from Hong Kong and specialises in authentic Cantonese fare, and this is their first Beijing outpost maintaining strict international standards.

### Il Ristorante - Niko Romito
Il Ristorante - Niko Romito earns 1 Star for Italian cuisine. It is priced Very Expensive at over ¥20,000. This dining concept by Milanese chef Niko Romito is the epitome of Italian luxe, lit by Murano glass and serving intensely pure, minimalist Italian plates.

### MO Jasmine
MO Jasmine holds 1 Star for Beijing Cuisine. The restaurant is classified as Expensive, ranging from ¥8,000 to ¥20,000. The dark green façade and warm grey room impart sophisticated taste, while the jasmine centrepiece on each table ties into its aromatic local dishes.
